# Beat UniPASE by keeping more, not generating more

No open model found clearly beats UniPASE on raw damage recovery at 48 kHz with a commercial licence. The way to beat it for podcasts is architectural: put a discriminative, mask-based model first so the voice is kept, let a generative model fill in only where the input holds nothing, and gate the whole chain so clean audio passes through untouched. Every 2024–2026 URGENT challenge points the same way. Discriminative systems kept speaker identity best. Pure generative systems scored highest on MOS while hallucinating content and losing identity. The leading URGENT 2026 systems were hybrids ([URGENT 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13531); [URGENT 2025](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/saijo25_interspeech.pdf)). Two new models are worth benching against UniPASE directly. **Sidon** is MIT-licensed, 48 kHz and 250M parameters, with self-reported speaker similarity of 0.961. **NVIDIA RE-USE** is discriminative, 9.6M parameters, runs at 8–48 kHz and covers every URGENT damage type including codec and packet loss, but its licence is **non-commercial**. Four of the project's gaps have no ready open answer: low-frequency bandwidth extension, repairing PCM audio that Opus has already concealed, heavy declipping at 48 kHz, and dereverberation of real podcast rooms. For those the bench has to build a degradation or a model, not just download one. The ranked shortlist is: Sidon; a discriminative branch (RE-USE as yardstick, MossFormer2_SE_48K for production); cheap identity diagnostics, including a same-file spectral-envelope match; a quality gate with an identity accept/reject check; and WPE plus SGMSE+ for dereverberation.

## UniPASE loses identity at its bottleneck, and its own paper shows it

earshot's measurement that UniPASE harms clean audio is consistent with what its authors report. **On clean input, UniPASE's own ablation gives PESQ 3.47 and speaker similarity 0.94**, so resynthesis costs about 0.06 cosine when there is nothing to restore ([UniPASE](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14606)). The model runs a distilled WavLM (DeWavLM-Omni), an acoustic stage and a vocoder at 16 kHz internally, then a PostNet to 48 kHz ([UniPASE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14606)). Miipher-2 shows the same pattern at a larger scale: **SpkSim 0.744 against 0.945 for the predictive TF-GridNet** ([Miipher-2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04457v3)). Identity is lost in the SSL-feature bottleneck and the vocoder, whatever the damage. Any model that reconstructs speech from WavLM, w2v-BERT or USM features inherits this risk. That includes Sidon, Miipher, GenSE and LLaSE-G1.

Self-reported speaker similarity is not comparable between papers. LLaSE-G1 reports 0.993 for itself, but UniPASE's independent test measured it at **0.77 with 12.15% dWER** ([LLaSE-G1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00493v1); [UniPASE](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14606)). Papers use different embedders: RawNet3 for UniPASE, WavLM for AnyEnhance, ECAPA for Voice-ENHANCE. earshot's WeSpeaker ResNet34 probe is the only number that compares these models with each other.

UniPASE's paper also claims first place in the URGENT 2026 objective evaluation. The official final ranking puts baird, subatomicseer and Ali-Universal-SE at the top of the subjective ranking. It also disqualified the top two instrumental entries for multiple registrations ([final ranking](https://urgent-challenge.github.io/urgent2026/assets/files/FinalRankingDetails_URGENT2026.pdf)). It could not be confirmed which team those entries belonged to, so the claim should be treated as unverified.

## Two universal models are worth a head-to-head; most others fail on rate or licence

**Sidon** (SaruLab, 2025) is an open re-creation of Miipher-2. It uses w2v-BERT 2.0 features with LoRA fine-tuning and a HiFi-GAN vocoder, outputs **48 kHz**, and is licensed **MIT** for both code and weights ([arXiv 2509.17052](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17052v1); [HF](https://huggingface.co/sarulab-speech/sidon-v0.1)). It reports **SpkSim 0.961 against Miipher's 0.930** on LibriTTS test-other, and runs about **3,390× faster than real time** on an H200. Its WER is slightly worse than the noisy input's (0.095 against 0.079), and the paper does not discuss hallucination or drift. Sidon was built to clean datasets, so its default is to resynthesise everything. Expect the same harm to clean audio as UniPASE until the bench shows otherwise.

**NVIDIA RE-USE** (March 2026) is the strongest discriminative candidate. It is a convolutional encoder/decoder with a bidirectional Mamba, **9.6M parameters**, takes mono input at 8–48 kHz, and handles noise, reverb, clipping, bandwidth limits, codec, packet loss and poor microphone response. It ships a chunked inference script ([HF nvidia/RE-USE](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/RE-USE)). It claims state of the art on the URGENT 2025 non-blind test. Its paper also finds that time-shifted anechoic speech is a better dereverberation target than early-reflection speech ([arXiv 2603.02641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02641)). Its licence, **NSCLv1, is research only**. That makes it a bench yardstick: if RE-USE recovers most of what UniPASE does while keeping identity, the hybrid route is proven and a commercially licensed equivalent becomes worth building or finding.

Only one permissive 48 kHz discriminative model was found: **MossFormer2_SE_48K** in ClearerVoice-Studio (Apache-2.0 code, `pip install clearvoice`). The licence of its weights was not checked separately ([ClearerVoice-Studio](https://github.com/modelscope/ClearerVoice-Studio)). It has no dereverberation or declipping model, and like DeepFilterNet3 it may remove speech along with reverb.

The rest of the field falls away on one filter or another. **GenSE, LLaSE-G1 and SenSE are 16 kHz only** ([GenSE](https://github.com/yaoxunji/gen-se); [LLaSE-G1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00493v1); [SenSE](https://github.com/ASLP-lab/SenSE)). **AnyEnhance** runs at 44.1 kHz and reports SpkSim 0.955, but its full 363M weights could not be confirmed (the HF URL returned 401). Only a 45.7M variant trained on 200 h is clearly available ([arXiv 2501.15417](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15417v2); [AnyEnhance-v1](https://github.com/viewfinder-annn/AnyEnhance-v1)). **Miipher-2, MaskSR/MaskSR2, Genhancer, SpeechFlow, VoiceBridge and Stream.FM** have no code or weights. VoiceBridge, a one-step 48 kHz latent bridge, is the one to watch for a release ([arXiv 2509.25275](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25275)). **Resemble Enhance** (MIT, 44.1 kHz) and **VoiceFixer** (MIT, 44.1 kHz) are older. In Sidon's data-cleaning test, VoiceFixer-cleaned training data scored MOS 3.771 against Sidon's 4.248 ([arXiv 2509.17052](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17052v1)). **VoiceRestore** (MIT) does not document its sample rate, and its card says it is "still in the process of training" ([HF](https://huggingface.co/jadechoghari/VoiceRestore)).

## Identity needs a gate, a reference and a second opinion

Conditioning a generative model on clean speech from the same speaker helps less than hoped. AnyEnhance's 3-second in-context prompt raises similarity by only **+0.006 to +0.013** (0.955 → 0.963 on Librivox), which is an order of magnitude smaller than earshot's measured UniPASE loss of 0.03–0.16 ([arXiv 2501.15417](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.15417)). Voice-ENHANCE adds an ECAPA-conditioned voice-conversion stage, fed with short clean enrollment clips that need not match the content. It reports **no speaker-similarity numbers at all**, and no code was found ([arXiv 2505.15254](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2505.15254)). No paper builds a speaker profile from the clean stretches of the same long recording and measures identity. The nearest prior work weights frames by a "cleanliness score" to personalise a denoiser ([arXiv 2104.02018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.02018)), and NANSY adapts at test time from a single sample ([arXiv 2110.14513](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.14513)). A per-speaker, same-file profile would therefore be new work.

The most useful lead points somewhere cheaper. **Speaker embeddings mostly encode static spectral information**, such as mean pitch, HNR and spectral tilt, and **equalisation changes alter similarity scores strongly** ([arXiv 2507.02176](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02176v1)). Part of UniPASE's 0.03–0.16 cosine drop could therefore be spectral tilt rather than lost identity. That can be tested in an afternoon. Take the long-term average spectrum of the speaker's clean stretches in the same file, match the restored segments' envelope to it, and re-run the speaker probe. If cosine recovers, a compact parametric voice prior has been found: a few dozen envelope numbers per speaker, measured from the recording itself. The same paper notes that embeddings ignore rhythm and proposes U3D, a rhythm metric (Wasserstein 2.15 for the same speaker against 21.53 for random speakers). The DDSP source-filter enhancer from Meta uses mel envelope, periodicity and F0 as its whole synthesis representation in 300K–600K parameters, but reports no speaker similarity and has no code ([arXiv 2508.14709](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14709)).

Before trusting a single cosine, earshot should get a second embedder. **ReDimNet (MIT) degrades least under codec and sampling-rate shifts.** TitaNet's EER rises 102% and ECAPA's 57% when going from 16 kHz to 8 kHz bandwidth ([Interspeech 2025](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/ferrofilho25_interspeech.pdf); [ReDimNet](https://github.com/IDRnD/redimnet)). RawNet3 would make earshot's numbers comparable with UniPASE's and URGENT's. Every common speaker embedder runs at 16 kHz and is blind above 8 kHz, so full-band identity cues such as sibilance and air need a home-made probe, for example LSD above 8 kHz against the same speaker's clean stretches.

Content hallucination needs its own metric, because MOS predictors do not see it. In URGENT 2025 the pure generative T13 had the **highest MOS (3.69) and the lowest speaker similarity (0.47, below the noisy input's 0.55)**. Its Japanese character accuracy fell to 36.8% from the input's 74.5%, and its output on unseen Japanese "sometimes resembled English" ([Saijo et al.](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/saijo25_interspeech.pdf)). A 2026 comparison found that **DNSMOS showed "minimal differentiation"** while phoneme metrics degraded ([arXiv 2606.02913](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02913)). The URGENT metric scripts (Apache-2.0), VERSA (Apache-2.0) and DiscreteSpeechMetrics (MIT; LPS and SpeechBERTScore) can be dropped in ([URGENT 2025 repo](https://github.com/urgent-challenge/urgent2025_challenge); [VERSA](https://github.com/ftshijt/versa); [DiscreteSpeechMetrics](https://github.com/Takaaki-Saeki/DiscreteSpeechMetrics)). Podcast material has no clean reference, so these would be computed against the damaged input. No paper validates that use, so it is a probe the bench has to calibrate itself.

For the gate, the licence-clean options are **UTMOSv2 (MIT)** and **Distill-MOS (MIT, 16 kHz)**, with **DNSMOS P.835** splitting SIG from BAK ([UTMOSv2](https://github.com/sarulab-speech/UTMOSv2); [Distill-MOS](https://github.com/microsoft/Distill-MOS); [DNS-Challenge](https://github.com/microsoft/DNS-Challenge)). NISQA is the only full-band estimator, but its weights are **CC BY-NC-SA** ([NISQA](https://github.com/gabrielmittag/NISQA)). UTMOS and SCOREQ ranked systems most like human MOS in URGENT 2024 ([arXiv 2506.01611](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01611)). The only precedent for routing between enhancers by predicted quality is SSEMS (2019). It beat a single general model "particularly under high SNR", which is the clean-microphone case ([Zezario et al.](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2019/zezario19_interspeech.html)). "Do no harm" is not a hypothetical concern: in one medical-ASR study the unprocessed audio beat enhanced audio in all 40 configurations ([arXiv 2512.17562](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17562v1)). Applying only part of the correction can also win: Whisper WER fell from 6.44 to 5.57 at mask strength α = 0.25 and returned to 6.46 at full strength ([arXiv 2607.11157](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11157)). That result is for ASR, not identity. Still, a wet/dry sweep is cheap to run on the bench.

earshot's existing `router` already bypasses clean material: router(unipase) leaves clean input essentially untouched on pp53. But it decides on band edges only. UniPASE's value lies in noise, reverb and codec damage, where the edge stays high, so the router either passes that damage through or would need a second, quality-based trigger. That trigger is the missing piece. A generative result would then be accepted only if speaker cosine and LPS against the input stay within bench-derived limits.

## Each gap has a conservative baseline; few have a strong open model

**Dereverberation.** **SGMSE+ is the only open checkpoint found for 48 kHz dereverberation** (EARS-Reverb, MIT), but it needs about 50 reverse diffusion steps ([sp-uhh/sgmse](https://github.com/sp-uhh/sgmse)). **BUDDy** estimates a per-subband exponential-decay room model for each utterance. It matches or beats supervised baselines in mismatched rooms, which is the real-podcast case ([arXiv 2405.04272](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.04272)). Its public prior is VCTK-trained, and its licence and sample rate were not found ([sp-uhh/buddy](https://github.com/sp-uhh/buddy)). **nara_wpe (MIT)** subtracts a linear prediction of late reverb. It cannot invent or delete direct-path speech, so it is the safe first answer to DeepFilterNet3 eating speech along with echo ([fgnt/nara_wpe](https://github.com/fgnt/nara_wpe)). None of the three has published results on short-RT60, close-mic podcast rooms.

**Declipping.** The neural declippers found are all 16 kHz. **DDD** handles heavy clipping (trained at 3 dB SDR, tested at 1 dB) and beats A-SPADE perceptually, but much of its code is **CC BY-NC 4.0** through Demucs ([arXiv 2401.03650](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.03650); [stet-stet/DDD](https://github.com/stet-stet/DDD)). SPADE-family methods work at any sample rate and keep every unclipped sample exactly ([declipping2020](https://github.com/rajmic/declipping2020_codes)). earshot's cubic declip already covers short plateaus. A learned declipper only earns its place on long plateaus, and earshot has measured clipping in real material as rare.

**Packet loss and codec.** **FRN** is the fitting open option: 48 kHz and blind, so it finds gaps without a loss mask ([arXiv 2211.04071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04071); [Crystalsound/FRN](https://github.com/Crystalsound/FRN)). It was trained on zero-filled gaps, though, while a guest's Opus stream reaches the recording already concealed. No paper on repairing post-PLC audio was found. The ICASSP 2024 co-winner BS-PLCNet is 48 kHz but has no code ([arXiv 2402.16927](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16927)). **Opus LACE/NoLACE and Deep PLC/DRED only work inside the decoder.** They need SILK bitstream features and are wideband-only, so they cannot be applied to recorded PCM ([Opus 1.5](https://opus-codec.org/demo/opus-1.5/)). On PCM, the realistic route is a universal model trained on URGENT codec and packet-loss damage (RE-USE, UniPASE). First, though, the bench needs a degradation that runs real Opus encode, loss and decode with PLC. Without it, any PLC claim measures zero-filled gaps that podcasts never contain.

**Low-frequency bandwidth extension.** **No open neural model regenerates 0–300 Hz.** The literature stops at Pulakka's harmonic synthesis from 2011–12 ([Interspeech 2011](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2011/pulakka11_interspeech.pdf)), and AP-BWE (MIT, 48 kHz, 18× real time on one CPU) extends upward only ([AP-BWE](https://github.com/yxlu-0102/AP-BWE)). Retraining AP-BWE on speech high-passed at 300 Hz is the obvious build. First, measure how much low band VoIP guests actually lack: Opus wideband passes more bass than PSTN.

**Voice isolation.** **SAM-Audio** (Meta) separates by text prompt ("speech"), keeping every speaker and returning a residual stem. It comes in 500M, 1B and 3B sizes, and its licence **permits commercial use**, excluding some military and similar uses ([arXiv 2512.18099](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18099v1); [licence](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam-audio/blob/main/LICENSE)). It is a generative flow-matching DiT, so it carries the same identity risk, and its weights are gated. **BandIt v2** separates dialogue, music and effects with **CC-BY-SA-4.0** weights. It is mask-based, so it cannot invent phonemes ([bandit](https://github.com/kwatcharasupat/bandit)). RoFormer vocal models were trained on singing, and their licences are unclear. URGENT-trained universal models already treat music beds as noise, because URGENT mixes in Free Music Archive tracks ([Saijo et al.](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/saijo25_interspeech.pdf)). earshot's `dehum` already handles hum by demodulation, a better tool than any model found for it.

## Imitating dxRevive is feasible in principle, and the project rightly declines it

Black-box modelling of deterministic effects needs little data. Amp models train on about **8 minutes** of audio, and an LA-2A compressor was modelled from **10 minutes** ([DAFx 2019](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2019/DAFx2019_paper_43.pdf); [arXiv 2102.06200](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.06200)). A speech restorer is a different kind of system. Its output depends on what it recognises in the input, so probe signals would have to be realistic degraded speech. Its stochastic bandwidth extension would regress to a muffled mean under an L1 loss unless trained with distribution matching ([DMD](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Yin_One-step_Diffusion_with_Distribution_Matching_Distillation_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)). Students exceed their teachers only when they also see true clean targets or auxiliary losses ([ROSE-CD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05688)). A pure imitation would therefore approach dxRevive, not beat it. In the EU, observing and testing a licensed program is protected by Art. 5(3) of Directive 2009/24/EC, and contract terms against it are void ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32009L0024)). Functionality is not protected by copyright ([SAS v WPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAS_Institute_Inc_v_World_Programming_Ltd)). No case law covers training on outputs, and a free clone would substitute for a small company's product. That leaves the project's rule as the right one: **dxRevive is a yardstick for evaluation, never training material.** The literature adds a technical reason to the ethical one, since training on true clean targets is the only route that can exceed it anyway.

## Ranked shortlist: the next five things

| Rank | Integrate or try | Why now | What would change the decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sidon** as an engine, head-to-head with UniPASE on pp53, nine damages, and run through `router` | Only MIT, 48 kHz, weights-available universal restorer not yet benched. Its self-reported SpkSim (0.961) beats its predecessor's, and it is fast | If its speaker cosine drop is smaller than UniPASE's at similar recovery, it replaces UniPASE in the generative slot. If it harms clean audio as much, that confirms the bottleneck diagnosis |
| 2 | **Discriminative branch**: RE-USE (bench only, NC) and MossFormer2_SE_48K (Apache code), alone and as front-end to UniPASE/Sidon | URGENT 2025/2026 show discriminative-first hybrids keep identity. RE-USE covers codec and packet loss at 8–48 kHz in 9.6M params | RE-USE near UniPASE recovery with near-zero clean harm proves the hybrid route. MossFormer2 then shows how much of that is reachable with a permissive licence |
| 3 | **Identity diagnostics**: second embedder (ReDimNet and/or RawNet3), LPS/SpeechBERTScore against input, and an LTAS envelope match to the same speaker's clean stretches | Cheap, and decides whether the 0.03–0.16 drop is real identity loss or spectral tilt. The envelope match is the same-file parametric voice profile, testable without training | If the envelope match recovers most of the cosine, a post-hoc profile EQ becomes standard behind every generative engine |
| 4 | **Quality gate plus accept/reject**: UTMOSv2 or Distill-MOS and DNSMOS SIG/BAK per segment as a second trigger beside the band-edge router; reject generative output when cosine or LPS against input falls past bench-set thresholds; sweep wet/dry | The band-edge router protects clean full-band audio but cannot fire on noise or reverb inside a full-band signal. The gate is what lets UniPASE work on those without touching good microphones | Thresholds come from the bench. If no threshold separates helped from harmed segments, gating by MOS is the wrong signal |
| 5 | **Dereverberation**: nara_wpe first, then SGMSE+ EARS-Reverb (48 kHz); BUDDy if its licence checks out | The project's DeepFilterNet3 result showed masking removes speech with echo. WPE cannot do that, and SGMSE+ is the only 48 kHz dereverb checkpoint | A podcast-room reverb degradation from real measured RIRs must exist first, or the result measures synthetic rooms |

Next after these: SAM-Audio (prompt "speech", using its residual for a sum-back check) and BandIt v2 for music beds; a real Opus encode/loss/decode degradation before any PLC model; and an AP-BWE retrain for 0–300 Hz only if the guest material shows the loss.

## Conclusion

The survey changes the question. Finding a model that recovers more damage than UniPASE is not the problem, because none of the open ones demonstrably does. The problem is that every model that recovers that much does it by replacing the voice. Discriminative models cannot invent a phoneme and generative ones cannot help doing so, so the winning podcast system is a composition: keep the original, subtract what does not belong, generate only into emptiness, and verify identity before accepting anything. earshot already has two of those parts, the band-edge router and the speaker probe. The literature contributes the third, a discriminative front-end such as RE-USE or MossFormer2. The fourth, a gate driven by quality and content, is something earshot has to calibrate from its own data, because no published threshold transfers.

One cheap experiment matters more than the rest. If matching restored speech to the speaker's own long-term spectrum recovers most of UniPASE's cosine loss, then "per-speaker profile" and "compact parametric prior" collapse into a few dozen envelope numbers measured from the same file. That would be a contribution the literature does not yet contain. If it does not recover the loss, the loss is real identity, and only a non-resynthesising path will fix it. That would favour the discriminative branch for good microphones permanently.
